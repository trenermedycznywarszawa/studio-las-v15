# 01 Client Journey

## Purpose

This document defines the real Studio Las client journey.

It is not a UX flow.

It is not a funnel.

It is not a list of app screens.

It describes the human transition from uncertainty, pain, fear, or loss of capacity toward clarity, trust in the body, and long-term independence.

## Core journey

The Studio Las journey can be summarized as:

> From: "Something is wrong and I do not trust my body."  
> To: "I understand my body better and I know what to do next."

A more concrete version:

> From: "My knee hurts and I am afraid to move."  
> To: "I can move again, I know my limits, and I trust the process."

The journey is not completed when the client finishes a plan.

The journey is successful when the client becomes more capable, calmer, and less dependent on constant reassurance.

## Journey stages

### 1. Discomfort

The client enters with a problem.

The problem may be pain, weakness, stiffness, fatigue, loss of confidence, return after treatment, fear of injury, or a sense that the body is no longer reliable.

At this stage, the client often does not need motivation.

They need orientation.

### 2. Decision to seek guidance

The client decides that guessing alone is no longer enough.

They may have tried random exercises, YouTube plans, gyms, physiotherapy, medical consultations, or periods of doing nothing.

The key need is not another plan.

The key need is someone who can interpret the situation and choose the next safe step.

### 3. First contact and qualification

Studio Las should not accept everyone automatically.

The first contact should clarify:

- what the client wants to recover,
- what they are afraid of,
- what history matters,
- whether Studio Las is the right place,
- whether another specialist should be involved first.

A good product protects the client from a wrong process.

#### First-contact service standard

First-contact speed exists to reduce uncertainty, not to manufacture urgency.

- An approved channel may acknowledge receipt immediately. The acknowledgement confirms receipt only; it does not qualify the person, promise acceptance, or substitute for Damian's review.
- Damian should reply personally on the same working day when practicable and no later than the end of the next working day.
- Before a phone call, Damian agrees a suitable time. Consent to contact does not make an unexpected sales call the default.
- Studio Las may offer the earliest useful times, but it does not impose a universal 48-hour booking limit. Client fit, readiness, travel, and real availability take priority.
- If an agreed call is not answered, Damian sends one short context message and asks for a suitable time. Routine messages do not repeat pain, diagnosis, or other health-related content.
- After the call, Damian confirms the agreed next step on the same working day. The message explains the path; it does not exploit fresh emotion or create a forced decision.
- If there is no answer, one respectful reminder may be sent after two to three working days. After seven calendar days without a reply, the inquiry may be closed without prejudice; the person may return later.
- Phone and email are confirmed through the approved contact or booking flow before being relied on.

### 4. Diagnostic entry

The diagnostic entry is not a sales meeting.

It is the beginning of interpretation.

The trainer observes:

- how the client moves,
- how they speak about the body,
- what they avoid,
- what causes uncertainty,
- what is safe enough to test,
- what should not be pushed yet.

The goal is not to prove weakness.

The goal is to identify the first useful direction.

#### Appointment and decision continuity

Once a First Diagnostic Visit is agreed, the client receives a confirmation through the approved channel. A reminder should arrive around 24 hours before the visit and contain only practical information such as time, location, parking, and agreed preparation. It must not repeat health-related statements.

After the visit, Damian provides the trainer-reviewed summary and next-step recommendation within the time agreed with the client. The current seven-day program decision and deduction boundary is owned by `docs/governance/02_OFFER_AND_PRICING_GOVERNANCE.md`. The decision window protects informed choice; it must not become same-day pressure or a countdown mechanic.

### 5. First direction

The client leaves the first stage with a simple orientation:

- what matters now,
- what can wait,
- what should be avoided for now,
- what the first home guidance should be,
- what signal the trainer wants to observe next.

The client should feel less chaos, not more information overload.

### 6. Early trust

The first wins are usually small.

They may include:

- less fear during a movement,
- better understanding of pain signals,
- completing a simple home protocol,
- noticing that movement does not have to be aggressive,
- returning to a daily activity with more confidence.

Studio Las should protect small wins because they rebuild trust.

### 7. Guided repetition

Progress comes from repeated, interpreted work.

The trainer connects:

- session observations,
- home signals,
- client words,
- load tolerance,
- recovery,
- daily life function.

The client is not asked to manage the whole system.

The client is asked to follow clear guidance and report the few signals that matter.

### 8. Adjustment

The trainer changes direction when the signal changes.

Adjustment may mean:

- increasing load,
- reducing complexity,
- changing the exercise,
- pausing a goal,
- returning to basics,
- asking for medical or physiotherapy input,
- simplifying the home protocol.

A change is not failure.

A change is evidence that the process is alive.

### 9. Pattern recognition

After enough time, the process should reveal patterns.

Examples:

- what improves pain,
- what increases fear,
- which days are harder,
- what level of load is tolerated,
- what the client can do independently,
- where the next limitation appears.

The app may help record signals.

The trainer gives meaning.

The report shows the pattern.

### 10. Report and next decision

The report is not a certificate of completed workouts.

It is a decision point.

It should answer:

- what changed,
- what did not change,
- what we now understand,
- what risks remain,
- what the client can do alone,
- what still requires guidance,
- what the next step should be.

The report closes one cycle and opens the next decision.

### 11. Independence

The long-term aim is not permanent dependence on Studio Las OS.

The aim is a more independent person.

The client should gradually know:

- how to start the day without the phone,
- how to notice useful body signals,
- when to continue,
- when to reduce intensity,
- when to ask for help,
- how to maintain functional capacity.

The best journey reduces confusion and dependency over time.

## What this journey is not

The Studio Las journey is not:

- onboarding into an app,
- a gamified challenge,
- a streak-building process,
- a generic fitness transformation,
- a dashboard adoption flow,
- a self-optimization protocol,
- a mass program,
- a content drip campaign.

It is a guided human process.

## Journey doctrine

The client should feel:

- safer, not monitored,
- guided, not controlled,
- understood, not scored,
- responsible, not abandoned,
- capable, not dependent.

The trainer should see:

- the person,
- the context,
- the signal,
- the pattern,
- the next decision.

The app should remain almost invisible.

## Implication for Studio Las OS

Studio Las OS must not define the journey.

It must serve the journey.

Any OS feature should be mapped to one of these purposes:

- reduce client uncertainty,
- preserve trainer memory,
- record a useful signal,
- support report creation,
- help decide the next step.

If a feature only increases engagement, tracking, or screen time, it does not belong in this journey.
