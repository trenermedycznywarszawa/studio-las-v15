# 03 Coaching System

## Purpose

This document defines the Studio Las coaching system.

It describes the trainer-led process of guidance.

It is not a training split.

It is not a calendar template.

It is not an app workflow.

The coaching system explains how the client is guided through diagnosis, hypotheses, experiment, observation, correction, report, and next decision.

## Core principle

Coaching in Studio Las means carrying the process with the client until the client can carry more of it alone.

The goal is not dependency.

The goal is growing self-trust and functional independence.

## Coaching doctrine

The trainer is responsible for meaning.

The client is responsible for honest participation.

The app is responsible only for preserving selected signals.

Paper is responsible for carrying simple home guidance without screen dependence.

A good coaching system does not create more tasks.

It creates more clarity.

## Coaching cycle

The Studio Las coaching system follows this cycle:

1. First contact
2. Qualification
3. Diagnostic entry
4. Initial hypotheses
5. First guidance
6. Session work
7. Home guidance
8. Signal collection
9. Trainer interpretation
10. Correction
11. Report
12. Next decision

This cycle may repeat across 4, 8, or 12-week periods.

## 1. First contact

The first contact should identify the client's real problem and level of readiness.

The trainer should listen for:

- pain or limitation,
- fear of movement,
- prior attempts,
- medical or treatment history that may matter,
- expectations,
- urgency,
- whether Studio Las is appropriate.

The goal is not to convince everyone.

The goal is to decide whether the process can help.

## 2. Qualification

Qualification protects the method.

Not every person should enter Studio Las.

The trainer should be willing to say:

- this is appropriate for Studio Las,
- this requires another specialist first,
- this requires medical clarification,
- this is not aligned with the Studio Las model.

A strong product has boundaries.

## 3. Diagnostic entry

The diagnostic entry begins the coaching relationship.

It should produce orientation, not overwhelm.

The trainer identifies:

- the client's main limitation,
- the client's fear pattern,
- functional priorities,
- first movement observations,
- initial safety boundaries,
- the first useful direction.

The diagnostic entry should not pretend to be a full medical diagnosis.

## 4. Initial hypotheses

The trainer creates a small number of working hypotheses.

A hypothesis may concern:

- strength,
- mobility,
- balance,
- coordination,
- load tolerance,
- pain behavior,
- fear avoidance,
- recovery,
- confidence,
- consistency,
- misunderstanding of body signals.

The hypothesis exists to guide action.

It should be reviewed, not protected.

## 5. First guidance

The first guidance should be simple enough to execute and meaningful enough to create a signal.

It may include:

- one movement priority,
- one home protocol,
- one thing to avoid for now,
- one signal to observe,
- one expectation for the next session.

The client should leave with less chaos.

## 6. Session work

A session is not a workout delivery unit.

A session is a coached experiment.

The trainer uses the session to:

- test hypotheses,
- expose fear safely,
- build capacity,
- teach interpretation,
- observe response,
- adjust the plan,
- build trust.

The success of a session is not only completion.

Success is a better next decision.

## 7. Home guidance

Home guidance connects sessions.

It should be short, calm, and paper-first.

It should help the client continue the process without turning the phone into the coach.

Home guidance should answer:

- what to do,
- when to do it,
- what to notice,
- when to stop or reduce,
- what signal to report later.

## 8. Signal collection

The client should not be asked to track everything.

A signal should be collected only when it may help the trainer make a better decision.

Examples:

- done / not done,
- pain response,
- confidence level,
- unusual difficulty,
- simple note,
- session RPE,
- trainer observation.

The signal is raw material.

The trainer creates meaning.

## 9. Trainer interpretation

The trainer interprets signals in context.

A missed home protocol may mean:

- the task was unclear,
- the task was too hard,
- the timing was wrong,
- the client was afraid,
- the client lacked belief,
- the client had a life constraint,
- the plan was not important enough.

The app must never flatten these meanings into a score.

## 10. Correction

Correction is the normal state of coaching.

The trainer may correct:

- the goal,
- the exercise,
- the instruction,
- the dose,
- the paper guide,
- the session focus,
- the expected signal,
- the timeline.

Correction means the method is responsive.

## 11. Report

The report closes a coaching cycle.

It should not celebrate data volume.

It should clarify what the process revealed.

The report is used to make the next decision with the client.

## 12. Next decision

After a coaching cycle, the trainer and client decide:

- continue guidance,
- change focus,
- increase independence,
- reduce support,
- refer out,
- pause,
- finish the process.

The best next decision may be less Studio Las, not more Studio Las.

That is acceptable if the client has become more independent.

## Coaching boundaries

The coaching system must not become:

- a habit tracker,
- a motivation engine,
- a content drip system,
- an automated coach,
- a performance leaderboard,
- a client self-management dashboard,
- a replacement for clinical care.

## Implication for Studio Las OS

Studio Las OS should support the coaching cycle by preserving:

- hypotheses,
- session observations,
- home guidance assignments,
- short client signals,
- trainer corrections,
- report-ready patterns.

Studio Las OS should not create a parallel coaching system.

The client should experience guidance from the trainer and paper first.

The app remains a narrow recording layer.
